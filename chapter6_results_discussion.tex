\chapter{Results and Discussion}
\label{chap:results_discussion}

\section{Introduction}

This chapter presents the results obtained from the corrected
thesis-aligned simulation described in Chapter~5. The reported values were
generated after separating the physical network from the artificial
entanglement overlay, implementing valid underlying physical paths, applying
Werner-state memory decoherence, enforcing consumption-time fidelity and
deadline constraints, modeling probabilistic entanglement generation and
swapping, consuming stored ebits after successful service, and introducing
budget-limited probabilistic regeneration.

Every experimental configuration was evaluated using 20 independent random
seeds. Methods compared under the same configuration received matched
topologies, traffic requests, generation outcomes, and service random numbers.
The reported confidence intervals are two-sided 95\% confidence intervals.
Paired two-sided \(t\)-tests were used where methods were compared seed by seed.

\section{Implementation Validation}

Before running the full experiments, eight automated tests were executed to
validate the principal scientific and resource invariants. These tests verified
that:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item every spatial physical topology was connected;
    \item topology-derived physical distances were preserved;
    \item every artificial e-link was supported by a valid physical path;
    \item temporal fidelity followed the Werner-state decay equation;
    \item end-to-end fidelity decreased with additional swapping hops;
    \item successful service conserved stored ebits and memory capacity;
    \item probabilistic ebit generation conserved memory capacity;
    \item seeded traffic generation was reproducible.
\end{enumerate}

All eight tests passed. Small integration tests also confirmed that request
constraints, route feasibility, regeneration outcomes, resource consumption,
and metric collection operated together without runtime errors.

\subsection{Fidelity, Delay, and Route Length}

The Proposed method also achieved the highest request-weighted end-to-end
fidelity, \(0.8694\). The corresponding values were \(0.8501\) for Shortest
Path and \(0.8560\) for Fidelity-Based routing.

The request-weighted completion delay of the Proposed method was
\(0.0191~\mathrm{s}\). This value was lower than the Fidelity-Based delay of
\(0.0229~\mathrm{s}\) and slightly lower than the Shortest-Path delay of
\(0.0207~\mathrm{s}\). The result indicates that the proposed method did not
obtain higher acceptance by selecting arbitrarily long routes. Instead, its
consumption-time prediction and coherence-budget filtering avoided routes whose
expected completion time would make their stored entanglement unusable.

The request-weighted mean hop counts were \(1.444\), \(1.595\), and \(1.380\)
for Shortest Path, Fidelity-Based, and Proposed routing, respectively. The
Proposed method therefore achieved the smallest accepted-route hop count in the
corrected experiment.

\subsection{Statistical Significance}

Paired tests were conducted on the seed-level acceptance ratios. The comparison
between Proposed and Shortest Path produced

\begin{equation}
t(19)=10.622,
\qquad
p=1.97\times10^{-9}.
\end{equation}

The comparison between Proposed and Fidelity-Based routing produced

\begin{equation}
t(19)=11.138,
\qquad
p=9.03\times10^{-10}.
\end{equation}

Both results satisfy \(p<0.001\). Consequently, the observed acceptance
improvements are highly statistically significant under the evaluated
configuration.

\section{Discussion of the Main Findings}

The corrected experiments support five principal findings.

First, the Proposed method achieved substantially higher request acceptance than
both baseline methods while maintaining the highest weighted fidelity. The
paired tests indicate that these improvements were highly significant.

Second, the benefit did not arise solely from fidelity-based path selection.
Fidelity-Based routing only slightly improved acceptance over Shortest Path.
The complete method benefited from combining predicted fidelity, entanglement
age, coherence budget, structural risk, route-level feasibility, and
regeneration.

Third, regeneration was essential under short coherence times. The ablation
study showed that Adaptive-Only routing provided only a small improvement,
whereas the complete method produced a much larger and statistically significant
gain.

Fourth, coherence time and regeneration budget interacted strongly. Short
coherence times created high regeneration demand, while longer coherence times
reduced this demand. Increasing regeneration budget improved acceptance, but
the gain saturated beyond five attempts per slot in the evaluated setting.

Fifth, the feasibility mechanism preserved accepted-route fidelity across
network sizes. Acceptance decreased with network size, but weighted fidelity
remained approximately stable. The method therefore rejected temporally unsafe
requests rather than accepting them with poor fidelity.

\section{Limitations}

The corrected evaluation has several limitations.

\begin{itemize}
    \item The simulator is a discrete-time abstract network simulator rather
    than a hardware-level quantum-network emulator.
    \item Physical-link parameters are based on controlled analytical ranges
    rather than measurements from a specific deployed platform.
    \item Purification delay was configured as zero and purification operations
    were not modeled explicitly.
    \item Requests demanded one end-to-end entangled pair; multi-pair requests
    require further evaluation.
    \item Node placement was uniform in a square region and did not include
    geographic obstacles or heterogeneous repeater hardware.
    \item Traffic was spatially uniform and did not include hotspot or
    application-correlated demand.
    \item Only the experiments reported in this chapter were executed under the
    corrected lifecycle. Earlier prototype-only scenarios were excluded from
    the final claims.
    \item Although 20 seeds support paired statistical analysis, more seeds and
    alternative parameter regimes would improve external validity.
\end{itemize}

These limitations restrict the scope of the conclusions. The results support
the effectiveness of the proposed method under the stated simulation model;
they do not establish universal superiority for every quantum-network
architecture.

\section{Chapter Summary}

This chapter presented the corrected experimental results. The Proposed method
achieved a mean baseline acceptance ratio of \(0.1052\), compared with
\(0.0404\) for Shortest Path and \(0.0416\) for Fidelity-Based routing. Its
weighted fidelity was \(0.8694\), and both paired baseline comparisons produced
\(p<0.001\).

The ablation study demonstrated a statistically significant contribution from
the complete adaptive-routing and regeneration mechanism. Coherence-time and
regeneration-budget experiments showed the expected temporal-availability
trends, including diminishing returns beyond a regeneration budget of five.
The scalability experiment showed increasing computation time and decreasing
acceptance with network size, while accepted-route fidelity remained stable.

Overall, the corrected evidence supports the conclusion that temporal-state
prediction, coherence-budget-aware feasibility, and targeted regeneration can
improve fidelity-preserving entanglement distribution under finite memory
coherence and stochastic link generation.
